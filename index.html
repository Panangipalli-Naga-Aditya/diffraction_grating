Principle

A grating is a large number of equally spaced parallel slits. Light from every slit interferes. Bright principal maxima appear only where light from neighbouring slits differs in path by a whole number of wavelengths.

d sin θₙ = n λn = 0, ±1, ±2 … is the order. d = grating element (slit width a plus opaque width b).
d = 1/N = 10⁶/N nmN in lines/mm. Lines per inch ÷ 25.4 gives lines/mm.

Overlap of orders

Order n of λ₁ coincides with order n+1 of λ₂ when nλ₁ = (n+1)λ₂. In white light, red at 700 nm in 2nd order (1400 nm) lands where violet at 467 nm falls in 3rd order, so the 2nd and 3rd spectra overlap while the 1st is clean.

Lab manual

Aim

To determine the wavelengths of the spectral lines of a source (or the number of lines per mm of a grating) using a plane transmission diffraction grating and a spectrometer, in the 1st and 2nd order.

Apparatus

Spectrometer with collimator, telescope, prism table and vernier circle; plane transmission grating; sodium or mercury vapour lamp; spirit level; reading lens.

Formula

λ = sin θ / (n N), where θ is the angle of diffraction, n the order and N lines per mm.

Procedure

  1. Level. Level the base and the prism table with the levelling screws and a spirit level.
  2. Eyepiece. Focus the eyepiece until the cross wires are sharp.
  3. Telescope. Focus on a distant object so it is set for parallel rays.
  4. Collimator. Light the slit. Adjust the collimator until the slit image is sharp and vertical on the cross wires. The beam is now parallel.
  5. Least count. Vernier constant = value of one main scale division ÷ number of vernier divisions (for 0.5° and 30 divisions, 1 arc minute).
  6. Normal incidence. Read the telescope on the direct image. Turn it 90° and clamp. Rotate the table until the slit image reflected from the grating falls on the cross wires, then turn the table by 45° and clamp. The beam now meets the grating at right angles. In this simulator it is already set.
  7. Observe. Unclamp the telescope. Bring the cross wire onto each line in the 1st order on the left and note both verniers. Repeat in the 2nd order. Cross to the right side and repeat.
  8. Simulator. Choose a source and grating, move the telescope slider until the line sits on the cross wire in the eyepiece, then press Record reading.

Observation table

Use the table on the Experiment tab. Columns: line, order n, angle θ, sin θ, calculated λ, error against the accepted value.

Calculation

For each reading λ = sin θ / (n N) with N in lines/mm, giving λ in mm; multiply by 10⁶ for nm. Take the mean for each line from the 1st and 2nd order. Then error % = (λ_calc − λ_true) / λ_true × 100.

Result

Wavelength of the yellow line = … nm (accepted 589.3 nm). Green line of mercury = … nm (accepted 546.1 nm).

Precautions

  • Level the table and keep the grating plane perpendicular to the collimator axis.
  • Use a narrow slit and focus once, then do not disturb the focus.
  • Clamp the table before measuring and use the tangent screw for fine setting.
  • Read both verniers and avoid parallax. Do not touch the grating surface.

Sources of error

  • Backlash in the tangent screw, so approach each line from the same side.
  • Centring (eccentricity) error, removed by using both verniers.
  • Grating not exactly at normal incidence.
  • Wide slit, which broadens lines and blurs the cross-wire setting.
